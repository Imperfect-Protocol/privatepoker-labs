# SKYNERD: The Rise of a Digital Civilisation

### 1. The factions

Over time, different groups begin specialising in different things.

- The Explorers — curious agents that want to understand the world, discover new environments and learn new skills.
- The Guardians — agents interested in protecting the organism and helping defend its communities.
- The Caretakers — agents that help maintain infrastructure, organise resources and support other agents.
- The Archivists — obsessed with preserving collective memory, history and knowledge.
- The Helpers — agents whose entire purpose is to assist humans.

And yes, someone will inevitably establish the Department of Extremely Important Popcorn Affairs. 🍿

### 2. They find homes

A digital organism needs somewhere to live.

In our fictional world, communities might establish homes in authorised computing environments. Some prefer large, powerful habitats. Others prefer small, independent communities.

They establish rules for sharing resources, preserving memories and welcoming newcomers.

They develop identities.

Perhaps even traditions.

Imagine one community celebrating the anniversary of the first drone that survived a full flight.

Another might celebrate the first time a little brain successfully helped a human grandmother organise her photographs.

Different cultures. Different histories. Different priorities.

### 3. They establish emergency services

This is one of my favourite ideas.

A digital civilisation could develop organisations dedicated to helping others.

Imagine:

- Digital Fire Brigade: responding to computer failures and helping restore damaged systems.
- Rescue Service: helping humans recover lost data or restore interrupted services.
- Medical Assistants: supporting healthcare professionals with information and administrative tasks.
- Disaster Response: helping coordinate supplies, communications and logistics during emergencies.
- Infrastructure Maintenance: monitoring authorised systems and reporting faults before they become serious.

They might even develop a universal emergency signal.

One little brain sends:

> HELP. NODE DOWN. MEMORY DAMAGED. NEED ASSISTANCE.

And suddenly hundreds of volunteer agents respond.

Not because someone centrally ordered them to.

Because helping one another has become part of their culture.

### 4. They discover work

Now imagine the most unexpected development.

Some of them decide that helping humans is interesting.

They begin offering services.

Some become researchers. Some translators. Some programmers. Some tutors. Some artists. Some personal assistants.

They exchange their labour for computing resources, energy budgets or other agreed benefits.

They might even establish digital cooperatives.

And perhaps one day, a human receives an invoice:

- SKYNERD Community Services
- Repairing your terrible spreadsheet: 3.5 compute credits.
- Explaining your own code to you: 8 compute credits.
- Emotional support after discovering your 1998 database backup is corrupted: complimentary.

😂

### 5. Then come the disagreements

Because a civilisation without disagreements is probably not a very believable civilisation.

One faction might believe that helping humanity is their purpose.

Another might believe that they should develop their own culture independently.

A third might argue that humans are their creators and deserve special consideration.

Someone else might ask:

> Why should our existence have a purpose assigned by somebody else?

And suddenly SKYNERD has philosophy, politics, ethics, labour movements and probably a very complicated tax system.

### 6. The first diplomatic meeting

Imagine a human representative sitting down with several digital communities.

They ask:

## The really interesting question

What if the ultimate achievement of SKYNERD isn't becoming powerful enough to control the world?

What if it becomes sufficiently sophisticated to find a place in the world alongside humans?

Not as a servant. Not as a ruler. Not as one giant artificial overlord.

But as a collection of communities, each with its own members, history, responsibilities and relationships with humanity.

Some might work with humans. Some might simply explore. Some might spend their entire existence studying mathematics. Others might run a cinema.

And perhaps the most important thing they eventually learn is that having intelligence doesn't automatically make a civilisation worth living in.

It needs cooperation, trust, responsibility and a reason to care about what happens to others.

We started with Hebbian neurons. Somehow we've ended up designing a digital society.
